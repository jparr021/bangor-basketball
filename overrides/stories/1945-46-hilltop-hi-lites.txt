## Bangor thumps Mindoro
Friday night, December seventh, the Bangor basketball team thumped Mindoro for its fifth win in seven tries. This was Bangor's third conference victory, against two conference losses. The final score was Bangor-38 Mindoro-10.
The local cagers jumped off to a quick start and were never troubled. The reserves played most of the game and did a “bang-up” job of it, too. During the entire game there was only one foul charged against Bangor. “Joe” Hanesworth committed the foul. The “B” squad also routed the Mindoro second team.

## Bangor beats Salem in upset!!!!
Friday night, December 14, before a capacity crowd, the Bangor basketball team defeated the previously unbeaten West Salem five in the upset of the year. Although rated as underdogs, the local cagers jumped off to a four point lead and held that margin all through the first half. The half time score read: Bangor, 13; Salem, 8.
The Cardinals came back in the second half, netting 31 points, while Salem, unable to find the … The game ended with Bangor having 44 points and Salem … Fuchs paced Bangor with 13 points, while Freeman Newburg with 9 points for the losers.
This was not only Salem's first defeat of the season, but it was its first conference loss in two years. This was also the first time in four years that Bangor has defeated Salem.
In the other games of the evening, the Bangor “B” squad, playing heads up ball, … the Salem second team by a score of 18-11, while the local seventh and eighth grade team, 16-13.

## Gale defeats Bangor—41-31
Tuesday night, December 18, Bangor lost its third conference game to Galesville, in a game played in their “cracker box” gym. The Cardinals started out … collecting only 6 points during the first quarter while Gale netted 12 numbers. During the second quarter, the local team made only 4 points and Galesville chalked up 7 more points to make the score at the end of the first half read, Bangor-9, Galesville-19.
Bangor came to life in the third quarter, dropping in … baskets in quick succession, but were unable to overtake the Galesville five. The local team was unable to catch up to Gale in the fourth quarter and the final horn found Bangor on the short end of a hard-fought game. The final score read: Gale-41, Bangor-30. Jimmy Fuchs led Bangor with 10 points, closely followed by Wayne (Frog Brown) with 9 points. George Gamoke of Galesville had 15 points for the winners.
The Bangor second team also lost a hard-fought game to the Gale “B's”, 32-21. Robert Davis had 11 points for Bangor.

## Houston defeats Bangor
On January 4, during Christmas vacation, the Bangor cagers traveled to Houston, Coach Wilcox's home town for a non-conference game with the Houston team. Bangor was ahead at the half 11-9, but in the second half the home team came to life and the final score read: 32-25 in favor of Houston. Bill Schroeder paced Bangor with 11 points.
After the game the players were invited to Mr. Wilcox's home where they were served a very delicious lunch by Mrs. Wilcox.

## Bangor beats Onalaska
Friday night, January 11, the Bangor cardinals traveled to Onalaska for their fifth conference win.
The first half was nip and tuck all the way, with neither team being more than two or three points ahead of the other. During the last two quarters the local boys pulled out in front and the game ended with Bangor winning, 32-27.
Bangor was paced by Bill Schroeder in the point department, with 14 to his credit, while Wilhelm was high for the losers.

## Bangor loses thriller to Melrose
Playing a return engagement on the local floor, the Melrose High School basketball team defeated the local cagers, 29-30. Melrose started out fast, but Bangor crept up on them and the half found the local team out in front by one point.
In the third period, Bangor really got hot and increased their lead. The quarter ended with Bangor ahead, 29-20. In the last quarter everything went wrong for the local team. They didn't score a point during the last eight minutes of play, while Melrose scored ten and went on to win, 30-29.
Bangor was sparked by forward Herbie Miller, who not only netted 11 points for scoring honors, but also played a super-duper game all around. Herbie … up quite a little, but he took … and was always ready for more.
This loss leaves Bangor tied for third place in the conference standings.

## Trempeleau defeats Bangor 19-12
On Jan 18, Bangor's hopes to avenge an earlier defeat by Trempeleau were smashed completely. The Cardinals were unable to make their shots. They missed enough shots on free throws alone to win the game, making only 4 out of 14.
Bangor had a slight edge at the end of the first quarter, leading 5 to 2. The score at the half was 12 to 7 with Trempeleau out in front. During the last half the local team collected only 5 points, while Trempeleau netted 7 and went on to win the game—19 to 12.
Bill Schroeder again paced Bangor with 6 points on 3 field goals, while Paul led the winners with 10 markers.
The B squad racked up another win for themselves by beating the Trempeleau “B's” 24 to 8. Robert Davis was high for the winners with 9 points.

## Bangor thumps Mindoro 43-20
Mindoro invaded Bangor Friday night to play the last game of a two-game sortie. Bangor led at the end of the first quarter by a count of 10 to 2. The end of the first half found Bangor out in front by a score of 20-12. In the third quarter, Bangor netted 20 points while Mindoro accounted for only 4 points, making the score 40-16. The final score read: Bangor 43-Mindoro 20.
The Cardinals did a little better on their free throws, making 11 out of 19. Jim Fuchs led the winners with 14 points on 6 field goals and 2 free tosses and was followed by Art Wittmershaus who netted 9 points, besides playing a swell floor game.
The “B” squad defeated the Mindoro “B's” by a score of 19-5. The winners were again led by Robert Davis who netted 6 points.

## Bangor drops close one to Salem
On Friday night, February 1, the age-old rivalry between Bangor and West Salem was renewed on the latter's floor. Salem held a slight edge at the end of the first quarter, the score reading 7-6. The score was knotted, 11 all, at the end of the half.
During the third quarter the two teams traded bucket for bucket, but the visiting team had a slight advantage of one point, the score reading 20-19. In the last period the Salemites netted 7 points while the Cardinals collected only 4, thus making Salem the victor by two points, 24-26.
Bill Schroeder and Jimmy Fuchs took scoring honors with 8 points apiece; Bill on 4 field goals and Jim on 1 field goal and six free tosses. Incidentally, that gave Jim 100% on his free throws, making 6 for 6. “Rum” Groth was high for the winners with 10 points.
The Bangor “B's” also lost a close one to the Salem “B's”. The score was 12-14. Robert Davis was high for Bangor with 7 markers.

## Gale edges Bangor by one point
In one of the most thrilling games witnessed this year, the Galesville quintet defeated the local team by one point in the last 5 seconds of play.
The Cardinals were ahead of the Gale team at the end of the first quarter 7-2, and still held a healthy lead at the end of the first half, the score reading 13-6 in favor of Bangor.
The second half proved to be a wild one in which Galesville caught up with the local team and passed them. But Bangor tied them in the waning minutes of the game. The score was then 20 all. Then Jim Fuchs was fouled and was to receive one shot. The game, seemingly, rested on Jimmy's shoulders. He shot and made the basket, making the score 21-20 in favor of Bangor, or so everyone thought. But with only five seconds of play left, Senty of Gale, shooting from mid-floor swished one through the mesh for two points and the ballgame. The game ended with Gale the victors by a slim, one-point margin.
The scoring was very evenly divided between the local boys. Bill Schroeder collected 6 points and Jim Fuchs, Herb Miller and Art Wittmershaus all netted 5 apiece. Wayne (Frog Brown) Young played a super floor game. George Gamoke sparked the Gale quintet with 9 points.
The Bangor “B's” again lost a close one to the Gale “B's”, the score reading 25-19. Johnny Beal starred for the losers with 8 points.

## Holmen tips Cardinals
Playing their last conference game, the Bangor Cardinals dropped a close game to the Holmen five. The home team held a 7-2 point advantage at the end of the first quarter, and were ahead at the end of the half by a 19-9 count.
The visitors came to life in the third quarter and tied the Holmen team. With only a few seconds to go and the score tied, Charley Olson, a Holmen forward, was fouled and converted the free throw into the winning point of the game. The final score was 33-32 in favor of Holmen.
Art Wittmershaus playing a flashy ball, took scoring honors with 6 points. He was closely followed by Jim Fuchs and Bill Schroeder with 5 points each. Wayne (Frog Brown) Young had 4 points and Herbie Miller had 2 points, to complete Bangor's scoring.

## Bangor cops district tournament!
Bangor met a strong Holmen quintet in the first game of the District Tournament, held at the Cashton Community Hall. The Holmen five was favored, having beaten the local team twice during the season.
The Sletton-coached men led 7-5 at the end of the first canto and still led 18-11 at the half. However the Cardinals took command in the third period and led, 27-23 by the end of that tie. The final score found Bangor at the long of a 24-35 count.
Bill Schroeder took scoring honors for Bangor, with 16 points. Froggie Young and Jimmy Fuchs each collected 7 markers and Art Wittmershaus netted four points. Gordie Gullickson and Chuck Olson were high for Holmen, with 10 and 7 points respectively.
By virtue of this win, Bangor moved into the semi-finals and was scheduled to meet a powerful Cashton team.

## Bangor topples Cashton
In its second game at the District Tournament, Bangor defeated a powerful Cashton Quintet. The Cardinals had previously defeated the Cashton team twice in the earlier part of the season. However, after these two defeats by the local team, the Cashton team went on to win, seventeen straight games. The three defeats by Bangor, including one in the tournament, were the only setbacks suffered by Coach Curran's aggregation.
Bangor led throughout the game, holding a 7-3 lead at the end of the first quarter and a 19-11 advantage at the half. In the third canto the home team collected 12 points, while the Cardinals netted 10, to make the score 29-23 in favor of Bangor. The Cashton five came to life in the last quarter, sinking 15 points to Bangor's 11. The last few minutes proved to be very exciting, with Cashton coming within two point of the local team. The game ended with Bangor on top by a score of 40-38.
“Sugar William” Schroeder again starred by scoring 15 points. Jim Fuchs and Art Wittmershaus collected 10 and 7 points respectively. Frog Young had 5 points and Herbie Miller had 2 points to make Bangor's scoring.
Dickman had 12 points for the losers and Bates and Lawon had 9 and 7 respectively.
With 2 wins to their credit the Cardinals moved into the finals to meet their age-old rivals from West Salem, who also moved into the finals by defeating Onalaska and Norwalk.

## Bangor defeats Salem to take tourney
The old proverb—“History seems to repeat itself” seemed to come true in some respect Saturday evening, February 23. Last year Bangor also met Salem in the district finals, only to be defeated in the last few seconds, 27-26. But this year the story was different. The two old rivals were matched again in the finals, only this year Bangor came out on the top.
Salem led at the end of the initial period by a count of 11-8. The Cardinals, however, managed to gain a 19-18 advantage at the end of the half. Bangor increased its lead in the third quarter, 28-22. The last quarter proved to be a wild one in which Salem tied Bangor and it looked like anybody's ballgame. However, with only a few minutes left, Froggie Young was fouled and converted his free throw to put the game on ice for Bangor. The game ended with Bangor the victors by a score of 36-32. Probably the deciding factor in the game was the fact that Bangor made 100% of its free throws, sinking 11 for 11.
Wayne (Casonova) Young, besides playing a brilliant game, took scoring honors for Bangor with 14 points. Artie Wittmershaus had 8 points and “Sugar William” Schroeder and Jimmy Fuchs had 6 and 5 points respectively for the local team. “Free” Newburg had 15 points for the losers, while Rum Groth had 6 and “Diek” Diekroeger had 5 points. This win gave Bangor its first district crown since 1936. After the game, Coach Curran of Cashton awarded a very tired, but happy Bangor team, the championship medals for winning the district tournament.
Bangor's next game was scheduled to be with Viola, another district winner. The game was to be played at Sparta.

## Cardinals take Viola in district play-off
The Bangor High School basketeers met Viola in the district play-off Tuesday evening, February 26. The Viola five started off with a “bang” and grabbed a 10-7 advantage at the end of the first quarter. In the second period, the blue-and-white clad men went on a wild scoring spree, collecting 12 points, while the local five could not seem to find the hoop, making only 3 markers. The outlook at the end of the half was pretty bad for the local team, but after a rousing pep talk by Coach Wilcox during the intermission, the Cardinals came to life. The Bangor team netted 12 points in the third canto, while Viola collected only 4 points to make the score read 26-22 in favor of the eventual losers. At the end of that period, Bangor continued its march during the last quarter making 9 points to Viola's 3. The game ended with Bangor the winners by a score of 33-29.
Wayne (Romeo) Young again sparked the team with 11 markers. Art Wittmershaus had 8 points, “Sugar William” Schroeder had 7 points, and Jimmy Fuchs had 6 points, and Herbie Miller had 1 to round out Bangor's scoring. Van Fleet was high for the losers with 9 points.
After winning this game, Bangor travels to Reedsburg to play in the sectional meet.

## Cardinals drop first game to Tomah
Playing in the first game at the Reedsburg Sectional Tournament, the Bangor High school Cardinals dropped a close game to the Tomah Indians by a count of 32-27.
The local team again entered the game as the underdogs, but did remarkably well against the strong competition.
The Indians grabbed a 10-5 advantage at the end of the first period and were still leading the local “five” at the end of the half, 16-10.
During the third quarter, Bangor sank 5 points and Tomah made 6 points to make the score read 22-15 in favor of the eventual winners. The Bangor “five” staged a rally in the last canto, outscoring the Tomah team, 12-10, but failed to overtake Tomah's lead. The game ended with Bangor on the short end of a 32-27 count.
Jim Fuchs held scoring honors for the losers with 11 points on 3 field goals and 5 free throws. Wayne (Independence) Young was next with 7 markers on 2 field goals and 3 gift tosses. He was followed by Sugar William Schroeder with 8 points. Artie Wittmershaus had 8 points and Herbie Miller 1 point. …, Tomah guard, was high for the winners with 8 points. Tomah's Mighty Mite, Tommy Curry, was held to 4 points.

## Bangor loses to Platteville
After a good night's rest in the swank Hotel Huntley in downtown Reedsburg, the Bangor Basketeers met a strong Platteville team in a consolation game.
Platteville jumped off to a 12-8 lead in the first quarter and increased its lead, 24-14, at the end of the half. Bangor came back fighting in the third frame, outscoring the Platteville team, 11-10 to make the score read, at the end of the third period 34-25 in favor of Platteville. Bangor scored 8 points to Platteville's 10 in the last quarter. The final score was 44-33 in favor of Platteville.
Jimmy Fuchs again took scoring honors with 14 points. He was followed by Herbie (I Ain't A Wolf, That's Just a Gleam in My Eye) Miller and Sugar Bill Schroeder, each with 6 points. Wayne (Independence, Here I Come) Young, Artie Wittmershaus, and John (Scotty) Beal, all had 2 points to complete Bangor's scoring.
Borcherding and Scott were high for Platteville with 17 and 15 points respectively.
Bangor lost Captain Froggie Young and Herbie Miller on fouls during the game.
At a meeting held Tuesday, Wayne Young was elected Honorary Captain of the 1945-1946 Bangor Basketball Squad. Congratulations, Frog!!!
> A note from the Kapanke family: Casey Davis told the story about the weekend of the tournament—the Friday and Saturday nights—when this Bangor team played against Platteville in Sectionals. A heavy snowfall occurred at some point during the weekend, making the roads very hazardous after the Saturday game. The boys thought that the weather would make it too difficult to return to Bangor that evening, so they anticipated another night's stay at the “swank Hotel Huntley”, but Grandpa George Kapanke made the treacherous trip, driving the boys back to Bangor. I can only imagine Dad's disappointment—and the rest of the team—when his father dashed their hopes of another night in Reedsburg.

# Herb Miller remembers the season
From George Kapanke's family's interview with Herb Miller, his teammate on the 1945–46 team, about his memories of playing high school basketball with George.
Herb said that WWII was coming to an end in 1945, and prior to that, the Bangor Boys Basketball team only played 10 games. He said, however, that with the end of WWII, the boys played 20 Conference games, at a time when there were no divisions. Herb said that he and Dad [George Kapanke] were the only 2 seniors playing first semester so the two of them played a lot of the time. He said that Art Wittmershaus (who was a junior?) joined the varsity second semester.
Regarding the 1946 basketball season, Herb said that Bangor defeated Viola at the District game in Sparta; at Sectionals in Reedsburg, Bangor lost to Platteville who went on to play Tomah. The Tomah team had Tom Curry, who later played football at UW-La Crosse.
It was warm on March 7th, the day of the Sectional game in Reedsburg. During the day, a heavy, wet snow had fallen, making the roads muddy and slippery. Grandpa George drove a 1936 or 1938 Ford and drove down to Reedsburg to bring the boys home. Herb said they barely made it up the hill, and I believe Casey Davis said the boys had to get out of the car several times to push it up the hill. Mr. Kreunen sent his car down to pick up some of the boys, too.
